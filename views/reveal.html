Research study

The reveal

Here's what really happened on the four websites

The study was not really about reviews. We wanted to know whether people can tell when a website collects data about them, and whether a cookie notice changes that judgement. To test this, some of the websites ran real data collection in the background and some did not, and some showed a cookie notice and some did not. All four websites and businesses are fictional.

Everything below was collected by this study's own server only. Nothing was sent to advertisers or any other company.

Why we did not tell you the purpose at the start: if you had known, you would have looked for signs of tracking, and we could not have measured how people judge websites normally.

Real websites use the same techniques you saw here: measuring how you read a page, building a "fingerprint" of your device from its settings, and loading invisible images. A cookie notice does not tell you whether these are happening, and some websites keep collecting after you click "Reject".

Your answers are stored on the study server and will be deleted when the project ends. They are reported only as group statistics.

Your choices

  • Use a browser with built-in tracking and fingerprinting protection (for example Firefox with strict protection, or Brave).
  • Install a reputable content blocker such as uBlock Origin; it blocks many tracking scripts and pixels.
  • Keep your browser updated; vendors add new anti-fingerprinting defences regularly.
  • Remember that "Reject all" on a banner only covers what that website chooses to honour.
